Getting started

What is Dancing to Beat ALS?

A national fundraising competition presented by ALS Catalyst Foundation. Participating ALS charities run their own campaigns, their supporters and company partners fundraise for them, and eight charities’ dancers earn a place in a ballroom competition at a gala in Philadelphia in December 2027.

Who does what?

ALS Catalyst Foundation runs the competition, the shared fundraising platform, the brand, event-wide sponsorships and the live show. Each participating charity runs its own campaign, recruits its supporters and company partners, nominates its dancer, and keeps its donor relationships.

When and where is the event?

Philadelphia, December 2027. The exact date, venue and qualifying dates will be announced.

How can I take part?

ALS nonprofits can enter their charity. Everyone else can fundraise for a participating charity, donate or buy gala tickets.

For ALS charities

Which organizations can enter?

U.S. 501(c)(3) public charities with an ALS mission, and hospital or university ALS programs that receive gifts through their charitable parent organization. Organizers review every application.

How do we enter?

Complete the charity application: your organization, campaign lead, dancer and campaign goal. Organizers review it, confirm your division and send a participation agreement.

Is there a participation fee?

Proposed fees are $500 for Grassroots, $2,000 for Discovery and Care & Support, and $3,500 for Reach. The fee is an entry fee to the competition; it is never taken from gifts to your campaign. Fees are pending legal review: no fee is collected and no agreement is signed until counsel approves the terms.

Do gifts to our campaign come to us directly?

Yes. Your charity is the legal recipient of gifts to your campaign, including gifts made through supporter and company teams. A shared multi-charity fundraising platform is planned to process gifts and pay each charity directly on its standard payout schedule. ALS Catalyst takes no share of gifts to charity campaigns and doesn’t hold them. The platform is still being selected.

Who owns our donors’ information?

You do. ALS Catalyst receives a donor’s contact details only if that donor chooses to share them, never sells them, and never contacts your donors without your consent.

What if we don’t reach our goal?

Goals are aspirational. There’s no shortfall bill; falling short of the finalist minimum only affects finalist eligibility.

How do we choose our dancer?

You decide: by appointment, application or audition. There are no random draws. Dancers can be anyone connected to your mission, and no dance experience is needed. You can name your dancer after you enter.

Can companies support our campaign?

Yes. Company teams and matching gifts count toward your campaign total, and they never change your division. Companies can also sponsor the whole event; event sponsorships fund the event and don’t count toward any charity’s total.

Divisions

What are the four divisions?

Discovery: organizations or programs directly performing substantial ALS research. Care & Support: organizations or programs directly delivering ALS care, home and community support, navigation, mobility, assistive technology or caregiver services. Reach: other established ALS nonprofits with more than $2 million in annual revenue in any of the last three fiscal years. Grassroots: other ALS nonprofits below that line, including campaigns organized by families and community groups for an approved nonprofit.

How is our division decided?

Mission first, then scale. If your campaign mainly funds ALS research you perform, it’s Discovery. If it mainly funds ALS care or support you deliver, it’s Care & Support. Otherwise, revenue decides between Reach and Grassroots. Funding someone else’s research or care doesn’t qualify on its own, and for organizations that do several things, the campaign’s main purpose decides. Try the division guide.

Who makes the final call, and can it change?

Organizers make the final decision, and divisions are fixed once a charity is accepted.

Does a company partner or celebrity dancer change our division?

No. Celebrity, dancer, influencer and company involvement never affect a division. A Grassroots charity stays in Grassroots even with major company support.

What happened to the Enterprise division?

It’s gone. Instead of competing on their own, companies now field teams for a participating charity or sponsor the whole event.

Supporters and donors

How do I fundraise?

Pick a participating charity and start a page on your own, with family, with a community group or with your company. There’s no fee, and every gift to your page goes directly to that charity.

Where does my donation go?

Directly to the participating charity you choose, or to the charity a fundraising team supports. ALS Catalyst takes no share of gifts to charity campaigns. A platform processing fee is shown at checkout.

Can I give to ALS Catalyst Foundation?

Yes. Gifts to ALS Catalyst Foundation, including gifts to its Host Team, fund its operations and future grants. They aren’t counted toward any charity’s campaign.

Do tickets, votes and sponsorships count toward a charity’s total?

No. Gala tickets, audience votes and event sponsorships fund the event itself, such as the venue, production and livestream.

Is my gift tax-deductible?

Gifts are receipted by the organization that receives them. For gala tickets, the amount that may be deductible will be stated before sales open.

Finalists and the gala

How are the eight finalists selected?

Four Division Leaders (the top fundraising charity in each division), two Wildcards (the next two highest fundraisers across all divisions), one Community Reach finalist (the charity with the most individual donors) and one Organizer’s Choice, picked by the organizing committee under published criteria.

What are the finalist minimums?

Division Leaders and Wildcards need $25,000 raised in Reach, Discovery and Care & Support, or $10,000 in Grassroots. Community Reach counts donors giving $10 or more and requires at least $10,000 raised. Organizer’s Choice weighs a compelling ALS story, community impact and the diversity of the field, with a $10,000 minimum.

When are finalists chosen?

The organizing committee will set the qualifying window, leaving finalists time to train before December. Charities keep fundraising after finalists are announced.

What if our charity isn’t a finalist?

You keep every gift, you’re recognized as a participating charity, and you can keep fundraising right through the gala.

What is the Host Team?

Founder Shawn Sarbacker and Andrea Sarbacker dance for ALS Catalyst Foundation, which runs the event. The Host Team is shown separately from the charities, isn’t ranked against them and is eligible for dance awards only. Gifts to the Host Team fund ALS Catalyst’s operations and future grants.

Who judges the competition?

Judges will include Allen Genkin, who competed on Dancing with the Stars: The Next Pro. Additional judges, judging criteria and scoring will be announced. Dancing to Beat ALS is not affiliated with or endorsed by Dancing with the Stars.

What awards are planned?

Awards will recognize both the dancing and the fundraising. Fundraising awards go to charities. Final categories will be announced.

Do finalists get gala seats?

Each finalist dancer receives a complimentary seat for a +1, plus a caregiver seat for a dancer living with ALS. Everyone else, including charity teams, buys tickets.
