Getting started

What is Dancing to Beat ALS?

A team fundraising and dance event bringing people together to support ALS beneficiaries. Teams name a dancer, raise funds for their chosen organization, and participate in an event pathway that includes division recognition and dance opportunities.

How do I register a team?

Select Register a Team on the home page. Enter your team and sponsor details, captain information, dancing candidate, beneficiary, and fundraising goal. Review the information and choose a Team Dance Card. In this mockup, completing demo checkout saves the registration in the tracker.

When and where is the event?

The event date, venue, registration deadlines, and final schedule will be announced. Dates shown in proposed competition rules are not a confirmed event schedule.

What is my team’s home location used for?

Your city and state or province help people find and connect with your team. It appears in the team directory. Enter a general location, such as Philadelphia, PA, rather than a street address.

Can I jump between registration steps?

Yes. Select a numbered step at the top of registration. Your current entries are retained, and checkout checks required information before completing registration.

Divisions and placement

What determines our division?

The Team Sponsor—the person or organization forming and running the team—determines the division, not the beneficiary, dancer, or fundraising goal.

How do the four division tests work?

Apply these tests in order and stop at the first yes: a business sponsors, funds, or fields the team → Enterprise; the sponsor conducts ALS research or clinical care → Discovery; the sponsor is a national ALS organization, media outlet, or individual with a national public platform → Reach. All other teams belong in Grassroots.

Can a family team support a research hospital?

Yes. A family team fundraising for a hospital ALS center belongs in Grassroots. The hospital’s own team belongs in Discovery. A business-sponsored team supporting the hospital belongs in Enterprise.

Who makes the final placement decision?

Organizers assign the division when accepting the team, and that decision is final. The division stays locked even if fundraising results, dancer selection, sponsors, or beneficiary change later. Ambiguous cases go to the organizing committee.

What if I don’t know which division to select?

Choose Help me decide in registration, or use the division guide. It applies the sponsor tests in order and returns a proposed division for organizer confirmation.

Dancers and beneficiaries

Does our dancing candidate need dance experience?

No dance experience is required. The registration page welcomes first-time dancers through experienced performers. The experience dropdown is optional.

Who can be our dancing candidate?

The form welcomes people living with ALS, caregivers, friends or family members, colleagues of people living with ALS, and ALS advocates or philanthropic champions. Provide the dancer’s name, relationship to ALS, title if relevant, and a short bio explaining why they are dancing.

How do we choose a beneficiary?

Select an anchor organization or search the beneficiary list during registration. Each team chooses one beneficiary. The chosen beneficiary appears in the registration review and team directory.

What if our preferred ALS organization is missing?

Select Can’t find your ALS organization? on the beneficiary step. Provide its website, ALS mission, relevant contact information, and your details. Organizers must review and approve the organization before adding it. Submitting a request does not automatically qualify the beneficiary.

Where do team-raised donations go?

Team-raised gifts are designated to support the team’s chosen ALS beneficiary and are granted under the Foundation’s grant policy. Any fees will be disclosed before launch. Team Dance Card fees and paid table upgrades help cover ALS Catalyst Foundation’s event and operating costs and do not count toward the team’s fundraising total.

Is donor information shared with the beneficiary?

Only if the donor chooses. Each donor can opt in when giving to share their name and email with the beneficiary. A privacy policy will be published before launch.

Dance Cards and gala seating

What is a Team Dance Card?

It is the team registration fee. Team Entry covers registration without gala seats. The Team & Gala Package combines registration with reserved gala seats. Both include a Givebutter team page in the planned live event; the local mockup does not create a live Givebutter page.

What is included in Team Entry?

Team Entry is for Grassroots teams only. It includes team registration, a Givebutter team page in the live event, and the same competition eligibility once fundraising thresholds are met. It includes no gala seats. Draft pricing is $100 early or $150 standard. Finalist comp seats still apply.

What is included in the Team & Gala Package?

Team registration, a Givebutter team page in the planned live event, reserved gala seats, a planned social post from the event’s professional dancers promoting the team and beneficiary, and eligibility for the competition pathway under final rules. A commemorative gift pack is under consideration.

What are the Team & Gala Package prices and seat allocations?

Grassroots: $450 early / $600 standard, with 2 reserved gala seats (valued at $450). Discovery: $1,500 / $2,000, with 4 reserved gala seats (valued at $900). Reach: $2,500 / $3,500, with 6 reserved gala seats (valued at $1,350). Enterprise: $6,000 / $7,500, with a Tier 3 second-ring table of 8 reserved gala seats (valued at $5,000). These are planning values, not tax-deductible amounts. Early-rate eligibility dates are not yet configured.

Can we upgrade from Team Entry to the Team & Gala Package?

Yes, subject to seat availability, with full credit for the Team Entry fee already paid. The live upgrade process is not connected in this mockup.

Can Enterprise teams choose a better table?

Premium Table Selection offers Tier 2, second ring, for an additional $5,000; Tier 1, ringside, for $10,000; or Spotlight, ringside, for $20,000. Upgrades include the table location and matching sponsor recognition. The Presenting table is sponsor-only. Upgrade payments do not count toward fundraising, leaderboard position, or Draw entries.

How are table upgrades allocated?

Tables are first come, first served by payment date, subject to availability from the same inventory as sponsor tables. A cutoff 60 days before the event is proposed, not confirmed. Shared inventory and real reservations are not connected in this mockup.

Are Dance Card fees refundable or transferable?

The fee is nonrefundable. Gala seats can be transferred. Paying the registration fee does not add to your team’s fundraising total.

Do finalists get additional gala seats?

Yes. Finalist comp seats are additional to the Dance Card: a +1, plus a caregiver seat for a dancer living with ALS.

Fundraising and the dance floor

Can we choose our own fundraising goal?

Yes. Use a suggested amount or enter a custom goal. The default for a new registration is $10,000. A goal is a target, not money already raised, and is separate from the Dance Card fee.

What do the finalists do?

Each team names one dancing candidate. The eight finalist dancers are each partnered with a professional dancer, train before the gala, and compete in a judged ballroom competition on gala night.

How are the eight finalists selected?

Proposed: four Division Champions (the top-fundraising team in each division); three Draw winners, drawn from the remaining teams, with more entries for teams that reach higher fundraising milestones; and one Catalyst’s Choice, a high-impact team or community member selected last by the organizing committee using criteria published in advance. Fundraising runs for 12 weeks and closes about eight weeks before the gala; dates will be announced. If a finalist can’t take part, an alternate is drawn the same way. This format and the Draw mechanic remain subject to confirmation and legal review.

Who judges the competition?

Judges will include Allen Genkin, who competed on Dancing with the Stars: The Next Pro. Additional judges, judging criteria and scoring will be announced. Dancing to Beat ALS is not affiliated with or endorsed by Dancing with the Stars.

How would the proposed Draw work?

Teams earn cumulative milestone entries based on cleared fundraising: $2,500 → 1 entry; $5,000 → 2; $10,000 → 4; $25,000 → 7; $50,000 → 10; $75,000 → 12; $100,000 → 14; $150,000 → 17; $250,000 → 20. These are total entries at each milestone, not additional entries. Fundraising runs for 12 weeks and closes about eight weeks before the gala; dates will be announced. Alternates would come from the same weighted pool.

Can teams outside the finalists dance in the showcase?

Proposed: a showcase of about 24 dancers, with the top six eligible teams per division. Fundraising thresholds would be Grassroots $5,000; Discovery and Reach $10,000; Enterprise $25,000. Unfilled slots roll to the next-highest eligible teams overall. Eligible teams beyond the cap receive a Grand Promenade walk-on; every registered team appears in a video montage. These details are not final rules.

Using this mockup

Is checkout a real payment?

No. The card details are dummy test information. No money is charged and no real confirmation email is sent. The payment and emailed-confirmation messages are demonstrations.

When will our team appear in the directory?

After successful demo checkout saves the registration to the Excel tracker. The directory refreshes approximately every 15 seconds; you can also select Refresh teams. Organizer acceptance and final division placement are tracked separately.

How do I find a team?

Open Meet the Teams from the home page. Search by team, location, sponsor, dancer, or beneficiary; filter by division; and choose a sorting option. The top bar shows overall and division fundraising goals, with a subtotal for filtered results.

Why are some teams labeled Demo?

They are fictitious teams added to demonstrate search, filtering, locations, and fundraising totals. Their goals are illustrative planning targets, not actual money raised or real registrations.
